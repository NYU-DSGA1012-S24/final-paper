# Final Paper Requirements

Your final project group will write an ACL-style short paper during Examination Period.

## Deadlines

- The paper draft is due on **Saturday, May 4** at 9:30 AM.
- The final paper is due on **Tuesday, May 14** at 11:59 PM.

## Requirements

- Your paper must use the [*ACL Proceedings Template](https://www.overleaf.com/latex/templates/association-for-computational-linguistics-acl-conference/jvxskxpnznfj).
    - Please use `\usepackage[final]{acl}` or `\usepackage[preprint]{acl}` on line 9 of the LaTeX source.
- You have unlimited space for your references, contribution statement, limitations section, ethical considerations section, and appendices.
- Your paper must be at most 5 pages long, excluding the items listed above.
- Your paper draft must have the following items: title, abstract, introduction, related work, description of your technique or task (including any datasets you created), experimental setup, and at least one result.
- Your final paper must additionally have a conclusion section, which says whether your hypothesis was confirmed or not.
- For extra credit, your final paper may optionally include a limitations section and/or an ethical considerations section.

## Rubric

Your paper draft is graded on _completion_: you will receive full credit as long as it has all required components. 

Your final paper is graded by Sophie according to the following criteria. For the most part, points are assigned based on whether Sophie:
- understands your research question and hypothesis
- understands your conclusions (i.e., whether your hypothesis is confirmed or not and why)
- is convinced of your conclusions (i.e., whether your experiment supports your conclusions).

| Criterion | Points |
|-----------|--------|
| **Formatting** 
| Paper is formatted correctly | 20
| _Page limit exceeded_ | _−10_ 
| _Citations incorrectly formatted_ | _−10_
| _Missing contribution statement_ | _−10_
| _Missing abstract or conclusion_ | _−30_
| **Logic, Framing, and Writing**
| Paper has a concrete research question and hypothesis | 10
| Paper is well-motivated | 10
| Title, abstract, and introduction present a clear argument | 20
| Related Work connects your project to past research | 20
| Content is organized logically into sections | 20
| **Proposed Technique or Task**
| The proposed technique or task is backed by intuition related to the hypothesis | 10
| The proposed technique or task is explained clearly | 20
| **Experiments and Analysis**
| Experiments are well-designed | 10
| Experiments are implemented well | 10
| Experimental setup is described clearly | 20
| Experiment 1 results are described clearly | 10
| Experiment 2 results explain Experiment 1 results: is your hypothesis confirmed? | 20
| **Optional Sections**
| Limitations | 5 EC
| Ethical Considerations | 5 EC
| **Total** | **200**
